# Quest `10` — Fieldwork

The quest where the class becomes one instrument. Every student gets the same four sensors, agrees the same message format and logs the same quantities in their own home for a week, and the readings land on one dashboard. That is only interesting because the students are in different houses: the dataset has a bedroom that never gets aired, a kitchen, a flat by a main road and a house with a wood burner in it, and those differences are the subject. Both of the student's boards work here, and the split follows what each one is good for. The ESP32 from Quest `00` becomes the fixed logger: wired to the sensors once, plugged into a wall socket beside the bed and left alone for seven days. The Stick is the instrument they carry and the one that comes to class. Nothing needs charging but the Stick, and the logger never moves. The last two missions train a model on what the class gathered and then test it against the one home held out of the training set, which is where a conversation about bias stops being hypothetical and becomes a number the class can explain house by house.

**Required add-on:** the environment pack — a VOC and equivalent-CO₂ gas sensor, a temperature, humidity and pressure unit, a light sensor and a sound sensor, with Grove cables. The pack carries both kinds of cable: Grove leads that plug straight into the Stick, and conversion leads that land the same four wires on the Quest `00` breadboard. The class broker and dashboard are instructor-hosted, so no family runs a server and no student holds credentials for anything but their own device.

**About £29** at UK retail — gas sensor about £10, climate unit about £7, light and sound about £5 each, Grove and conversion cables about £4 — matching the Quest `00` kit and the Quest `01` pack.

**Instructor note:** the gas sensor reports *equivalent* CO₂ inferred from volatile organic compounds, not measured CO₂, and it drifts. Mission `011` handles that without anybody buying an accurate sensor, and without a class-shared one: the reference is outdoor air, which sits near 420 ppm almost everywhere on earth, so every student baselines against the same known quantity from their own window at the same agreed minute. A shared *condition* rather than a shared *object* — which is what lets the mission run for a class spread across sixteen houses. An in-person cohort with a lab-grade sensor to hand can use it as well, but no mission needs one.

**Builds on Quests `00` and `01`.** Both boards are in use and neither is spare: the breadboard ESP32 is the logger that stays put, the Stick is the instrument that travels. **One device to prepare:** the logger is a fixture on mains power that is set up once in mission `101` and never brought anywhere, so the only thing a student charges before a session is the Stick, exactly as in Quest `01`. **Carried forward:** the broker, the agreed message format and the calibration habit are what the Quest `11` build reports through, and the sensors stay available to it as a second input.

Mission `011` is the one that earns the rest. Sixteen students open sixteen windows at the same agreed minute and get sixteen different numbers for air that is very nearly identical in all sixteen places. The spread is not the weather; it is the sensors. Until each device's offset from that known baseline is measured and written down, the pooled dataset in mission `101` is uninterpretable — the cheapest possible demonstration of why real measurement programmes spend so much of their time on calibration.

**The unlock.** Mission `111` ends with the vision pack handed over, at the session where the class has just explained who its own dataset leaves out. Quest `11` puts the same question to a camera.
