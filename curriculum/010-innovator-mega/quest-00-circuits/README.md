# Quest `00` — Circuits

The board on its own, for eight missions. Nothing is assembled, nothing is bought and nothing moves: a student with the starter kit can do every mission on a breadboard at a kitchen table. Two things happen at once here, and they are meant to. The student leaves blocks behind for text-based Python, and at the same moment the program stops being a simulation of a device and starts being the device — a line they type turns a real pin on, and a wire they put in the wrong hole stops it working. By mission `111` they have written a program that runs on battery with no laptop attached, which is the moment the board becomes theirs.

**No add-on.** Everything in this quest runs on the Innovator starter kit: an ESP32 dev board, a USB cable, a breadboard, jumper wires and a handful of LEDs, resistors, a button, a potentiometer and basic sensors — about £30 at UK retail, and the figure every later add-on in this realm is sized against. A student whose kit has not arrived can follow the first missions in Wokwi, a browser simulator that runs MicroPython against a simulated ESP32 and breadboard. It does not cover mission `110`, where the faults being diagnosed are physical ones.

**Carried forward.** This board is the realm's *what is actually inside*. Quest `01` hands the student the same silicon in a case with a screen on it, and that comparison only lands because they met the bare version first — they know what is under the plastic, because they wired it. The board is not retired either: the four wires of a Grove connector are the four they strip here, and in Quest `10` this is the logger that stays plugged into a wall at home for a week. It has no battery and never comes to class, so it is never a second device to charge.

| Mission | Title | What the student makes | Concepts & standards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **[`000`](000-hello-repl/)** | [Hello, REPL](000-hello-repl/) | MicroPython flashed onto the board and a live prompt that answers back — the onboard LED switched on by a line the student types rather than a program they run | The REPL as a conversation with the board, not a program; `machine.Pin` — a pin is an object whose value you set; flashing firmware, choosing a port, and what to do when the board does not appear; CSTA 2‑CS‑02 |
| **[`001`](001-code-that-stays/)** | [Code That Stays](001-code-that-stays/) | The same blink written as a file, saved to the board as `main.py`, and running on battery the moment it is plugged in with no laptop attached | A script stored on the board versus a line typed at the prompt; indentation is syntax — the first thing text-based code demands that blocks never did; `boot.py`, `main.py`, and a device that starts without you; CSTA 2‑AP‑19 |
| **[`010`](010-numbers-that-mean-volts/)** | [Numbers That Mean Volts](010-numbers-that-mean-volts/) | A potentiometer and a light sensor read as raw counts, converted to volts, then mapped into a unit that means something — dark to bright, closed to open | Analogue input: ADC counts, resolution and reference voltage; converting a raw range into a real one, and naming the units; voltage dividers, and why a light sensor needs a second resistor; CSTA 2‑DA‑07; NGSS practice: **planning and carrying out investigations** |
| **[`011`](011-loops-conditions-functions/)** | [Loops, Conditions, Functions](011-loops-conditions-functions/) | The growing program restructured into functions with parameters, a `while True` loop that reads a button through a pull-up resistor, and a bounce the student can see and then fix | Functions, parameters and return values in text; `while True` with `time.sleep` — the shape of nearly every embedded program; pull-ups, floating inputs and switch bounce; CSTA 2‑AP‑14, 2‑AP‑12 |
| **[`100`](100-fading-not-switching/)** | [Fading, Not Switching](100-fading-not-switching/) | An LED that fades rather than switches and a buzzer that holds a pitch, both from the same PWM peripheral with duty cycle and frequency under the student's control | PWM: duty cycle versus frequency, and why the eye and the ear read them differently; Ohm's law in practice — sizing a resistor so the LED survives; current limits per pin and per board; writing down a power budget; CSTA 2‑CS‑02; NGSS core idea: **PS3B – energy transfer** |
| **[`101`](101-reading-the-datasheet/)** | [Reading the Datasheet](101-reading-the-datasheet/) | A sensor nobody has demonstrated, wired and read correctly — pinout, supply voltage and timing taken from its datasheet, with a driver library imported and credited | Reading a pinout, an absolute-maximum table and a timing diagram; using and attributing an existing library instead of writing one; 3.3 V parts versus 5 V parts, and what a level shifter is for; CSTA 2‑AP‑16, 2‑CS‑02 |
| **[`110`](110-when-the-circuit-disagrees/)** | [When the Circuit Disagrees with the Code](110-when-the-circuit-disagrees/) | Four boards broken on purpose — a floating input, a missing ground, a wrong pin and a brownout — each diagnosed in writing by a method rather than by guessing | A debugging method: check power first, halve the system, one change at a time; symptoms that look like software and are not; reading the board's own error output, and what a reset loop is telling you; CSTA 2‑CS‑03 |
| **[`111`](111-off-the-laptop/)** | [Off the Laptop](111-off-the-laptop/) | **Build and show.** A battery-powered device of the student's own design that senses one thing and reports it, boots from `main.py` with no laptop attached, and is demonstrated with its circuit drawn and one bug explained | NGSS practice: **designing solutions and communicating results**; STEL practice: **making and doing**; drawing the circuit you built so that somebody else could rebuild it |

Mission `110` is the one that separates this quest from a Python course. Every fault in it — a floating input, a missing ground, the wrong pin, a brownout under load — presents as a software bug and is not one, and a student who has met all four stops assuming the code is the problem.

**The unlock.** Mission `111` ends with the M5 pack handed over — the hardware Quest `01` runs on, opened at the showcase, everybody at once, in front of whoever came to watch. It is the realm's running joke made physical: the student has just built the inside, and is handed the outside.
