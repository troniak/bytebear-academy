# Quest `11` — Vision

The realm's capstone, and the only quest where the hardware does not say what to build. The Grove Vision AI module is a camera with a neural network processor behind it: the model runs on the module, not on a laptop and not in a cloud, and the host board only receives the answer — a class, a confidence and a box. That changes what a student can build. The first four missions assemble the chain: a stock model, a driver that reads detections into MicroPython, a dataset the student captures and labels, a model they trained and tested. The last four put it on a moving head and turn it into a machine a pod designed, another pod broke, and every student can explain.

**Required add-on:** the vision pack — a Grove Vision AI V2 module with its camera, a pan-and-tilt bracket, two micro servos and a separate 5 V supply for them. The whole thing runs on the Stick: the camera module goes into its Grove port, and the two servos take their signal from its header pins and their power from the battery holder in the pack — a separate supply sharing a common ground, which is the rule from Quest `00` mission `100` applied to something that would otherwise brown the board out. Models are trained and deployed through SenseCraft in the browser, with no code and no account beyond the class one.

**About £35** at UK retail — vision module about £15, camera about £7, pan-and-tilt bracket about £5, two micro servos about £7, battery holder about £2. It is the one pack that runs a few pounds above the others; buying the bracket unassembled is what keeps it close, and students build it in mission `100` rather than unboxing it.

**Instructor note:** the module's own library is written for Arduino. ByteBear supplies a tested MicroPython driver in the mission folders — reading detections over I²C at address `0x62`, with the documented UART protocol as the fallback when a board's I²C is unhappy. Confirm it against the firmware the modules ship with before the first session; students read that driver in mission `001` rather than writing it.

**Builds on everything before it, and runs on one device.** The Stick is the whole machine: Grove port for the camera, header pins for the servos, screen for what the model is seeing, battery so the thing can be picked up and carried to the front of the room. **One device to prepare** — the same one as the two quests before. The rest of what a student owns widens the brief without being required: the Quest `00` ESP32 can take a job as a second node over the Quest `10` broker, the environment sensors let a machine react to the room as well as to what it sees, and the micro:bit rover from the Maker realm lets it move without anybody buying another one.

The model runs on the module, and saying so plainly is part of the teaching. Nothing in this quest is sent to a cloud, nothing waits on a laptop, and the whole machine works with the Wi-Fi off — which is exactly what makes the Engineer realm's move to a Raspberry Pi legible. There the student writes the vision code themselves instead of deploying somebody's model to a module, and they will already know what they are being given control over.

**The unlock.** There is no box at the end of this one. Mission `111` closes the realm, and what it hands over is the question the Engineer realm opens with — asked while the finished machine is still running on the table.
