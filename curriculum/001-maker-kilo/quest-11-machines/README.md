# Quest `11` — Machines

The last Maker quest removes the kit. Every quest so far arrived as a product with an intended use; the inventor's pack arrives as a box of parts with no model to build, and the pod has to decide what the machine is for before anything gets wired. The first four missions teach the parts, the last four are the build — planned, prototyped, broken by someone else and rebuilt — and the realm ends with each student explaining their own contribution to a machine that did not exist eight weeks earlier.

**Required add-on:** the inventor's pack — an edge connector and breadboard, two servos, a gripper or arm bracket, a small set of external sensors, a buzzer, LEDs and crocodile clips. The rover and radio pack from Quests `01` and `10` are assumed to be on the table as well; most pods use at least one of them.

| Mission | Title | What the student makes | Concepts & standards |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **[`000`](000-beyond-the-board/)** | [Beyond the Board](000-beyond-the-board/) | An external LED and buzzer driven from the pins with crocodile clips, and a working explanation of what 3V, GND and a pin actually are | A pin is an output you wire yourself; circuits and the one rule about shorting power to ground; CSTA 2‑CS‑02 |
| **[`001`](001-reading-the-world-through-a-pin/)** | [Reading the World Through a Pin](001-reading-the-world-through-a-pin/) | An external analogue sensor read into the micro:bit, its raw numbers calibrated into something meaningful — dry to soaked, dark to bright | Analogue versus digital; mapping a raw range onto a real one; CSTA 2‑DA‑07; NGSS practice: **planning investigations** |
| **[`010`](010-angles-you-command/)** | [Angles You Command](010-angles-you-command/) | A servo driven to specific angles, swept, and stopped at its limits without straining | Position control rather than speed; degrees as a commanded value; NGSS core ideas: **forces and motion** |
| **[`011`](011-a-thing-that-grips/)** | [A Thing That Grips](011-a-thing-that-grips/) | The gripper or arm assembled and programmed to open, close and hold something real — heavy enough to be hard | Mechanism, leverage and torque; the gap between a design that works on paper and one that lifts; STEL practice: **making and doing** |
| **[`100`](100-designing-for-a-person/)** | [Designing for a Person](100-designing-for-a-person/) | A one-page design brief: who the machine is for, what problem it solves, and the test it must pass to count as finished | Requirements and constraints; agreeing "done" before building; NGSS practice: **defining problems**; STEL practice: **critical thinking** |
| **[`101`](101-build-one/)** | [Build One](101-build-one/) | A first working prototype with pod roles assigned — builder, coder, documentarian, presenter — and progress logged on the tablets | Prototyping and division of labour; CSTA 2‑AP‑18, 2‑AP‑19 |
| **[`110`](110-break-it-and-improve-it/)** | [Break It and Improve It](110-break-it-and-improve-it/) | Their machine handed to another pod to test, every failure logged, and one improvement made and measured against the brief from mission `100` | Testing by someone who did not build it; iteration with evidence; CSTA 2‑AP‑17, 2‑CS‑03 |
| **[`111`](111-showcase/)** | [Showcase](111-showcase/) | **Build and show.** The finished machine demonstrated live to families, with every student presenting their own contribution and naming one failure and what they changed | NGSS practice: **designing solutions and communicating results**; STEL practice: **collaboration & communication** |

Mission `110` is the one that distinguishes this quest from a build club. A pod that tests its own machine tests it gently and along the path it already knows works; another pod does not, and the failure log they hand back is the evidence the final improvement has to answer to.
