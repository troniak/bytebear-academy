## What we'll build

Teams start away from the screen, planning a route on paper, then translate that plan
into a coded sequence that steers a LEGO carriage past obstacles. In the second hour
they connect a colour sensor to a motor and choreograph a dance — the moment where
hardware and software visibly click together.

## What your child will learn

- Breaking a problem into ordered steps (`first`, `next`, `then`, `finally`)
- Predicting what a program will do before running it, then debugging the difference
- How a sensor and a motor work together as one system
- How software controls hardware

## How the session runs

Students work in pods of four with one LEGO kit and one device per pod, rotating
through Designer, Builder, Programmer and Tester-documenter so everyone codes and
everyone builds. The session always ends with a working project and a short demo.

The 4-hour version adds a lunch break and a ship-navigation design challenge, where
teams combine inputs, outputs and sequences to reach an island.

## What's included

Every pod gets a LEGO Education Computer Science & AI kit and a tablet. No prior
coding experience is needed — this is the starting point of the sequence.
